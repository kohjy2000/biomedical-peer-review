# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** Host control of latent Epstein-Barr virus infection
**Journal:** Nature — initial submission

## Overall Assessment

This study extracts EBV-mapping short reads from the genome sequencing data of 486,315 UK Biobank and 336,123 All of Us participants, treats read positivity (EBVread+) as a surrogate for EBV viral load, and uses it for epidemiological association, a well-powered GWAS, rare-variant and enrichment analyses, polygenic scores, PheWAS and Mendelian randomization. The scale, the QC discipline, and the resolution of the MHC signal into 54 independent HLA alleles are genuine strengths, and the ERAP2 haplotype analysis is a well-argued mechanistic vignette. Two load-bearing claims are not supported at their stated level. The evidence that read positivity indexes viral load rests on a simulation fitted to the distribution it is used to explain; and the disease-overlap and causal claims rest on an MHC-dominated instrument set whose alleles have established direct effects on the outcome diseases. Both are addressable, largely with data already in hand.

## Major Comments

**1. "Viral load" is asserted rather than benchmarked.**
The abstract states that "The detection of EBV-reads (EBVread+) reflected increased viral load". The support is Extended Data Fig. 1, whose legend states that the log-normal and binomial parameters were "manually fitted to match the observed read count distribution in our data", and Fig. 2b, in which both compared phenotypes derive from the same read-generation process. Neither separates viral DNA content from detection propensity, and the manuscript shows that detection is strongly depth-dependent (Fig. 1g: prevalence rising from ≈0.10 to ≈0.45 across GS yield) and that 51 library-preparation plates had to be excluded for excess positivity. Since 61.9% of positives carry exactly one read, low-level index swapping on patterned Illumina flow cells (Costello et al., BMC Genomics 19:332, 2018) is a live alternative contributor. Please provide either an orthogonal quantitative anchor (qPCR/ddPCR, or comparison with an independently deep-sequenced subset) or an in-silico decomposition feasible within the existing platforms: association results after down-sampling to uniform depth, an estimate of within-flow-cell read sharing that bounds index swapping, and EBVread+ concordance across any re-sequenced samples. If neither establishes calibration to viral copies, please rename the phenotype (for example, "detectable blood EBV DNA") and remove "viral load" from the title, abstract and conclusions; the GWAS itself would be unaffected.

**2. The causal and disease-overlap claims cannot be distinguished from HLA pleiotropy.**
Because the exposure GWAS is MHC-dominated, both the best-performing score (HLA_all) and the MR instrument set consist largely of HLA alleles with established direct effects on the outcome diseases: HLA-DRB1*04:04 is a shared-epitope RA risk allele (ref. 25), the class II alleles driving the RA signal sit on the DR4–DQ8 background associated with T1D, and the MS result is abolished by removing HLA-A*02:01, itself a known MS-protective allele (ref. 26). You report both significant instrument heterogeneity and that "the causal effects for these outcomes were driven by SNPs in the MHC region". The exclusion restriction therefore fails, and the RA/T1D causal claims are not separable from shared antigen-presentation genetics. Please report in the main text (i) the MR restricted to non-MHC instruments, which the Methods state was performed, with instrument-strength statistics for that set, and (ii) the score–disease associations conditioned on each disease's established HLA risk alleles, or restricted to non-MHC scores. Please also note that EBVread+ dichotomizes a latent continuum, so ORwMed = 1.86 for T1D has no defined per-unit scale (Burgess & Labrecque, Eur J Epidemiol 33:947, 2018). If the restricted analyses are null or weak-instrument limited, the shared-architecture result remains of interest and should be presented as such, with causal language removed.

**3. Specificity of the associated loci is inferred from two underpowered nulls, one trending against the claim.**
The statements that the loci "represent specific factors associated with EBV viral load during latency" and "do not seem to be confounded by memory B cell abundance" rest on two effect-size correlations across 27 points. The HHV-7 comparison gives r = 0.37 (P = 0.061), with six loci nominally concordant in direction and two showing shared causal variants — evidence of partial sharing, not of specificity. The memory-B-cell comparison uses a GWAS of 3,757 Sardinians (ref. 30) with no MHC data, so r = 0.34 (P = 0.112) is uninformative either way. This matters because read content is the product of infected-cell number and per-cell copy number, and the two B-cell-axis genes that emerge — the TNFSF13B locus and TNFRSF13B p.Cys104Arg, the only non-MHC rare-variant hit — are what a reservoir-size contribution predicts. Please state the power of each comparison given the external standard errors, add a sensitivity GWAS conditioned on measured blood-cell parameters, and reword the specificity claim to acknowledge the positive HHV-7 trend.

**4. The novel-gene and IEI claims exceed the statistics presented.**
Twenty-four genes overlapping between MAGMA and the rare-variant analysis at P < 0.01 are called "strong novel candidate genes", with no chance expectation given for an intersection of two thresholded lists across ~19,000 genes. Separately, the monogenic EBV-susceptibility set (beta = 0.35, s.e.m. = 0.22, P = 0.055) is described as showing that "the effect size further increased" over the broader IEI set (beta = 0.19, s.e.m. = 0.04), and the Discussion converts this into "a higher effect size for IEIs that lead to a loss of EBV-control". The two overlapping estimates were not formally contrasted. Please provide a permutation or matched-null test for the overlap and a formal contrast between the gene sets, or present CD226 and the other nominated genes as candidates requiring functional follow-up.

**5. Summary statistics and analysis code are not available.**
Data availability covers only the biobank individual-level data, and Code availability provides read-extraction commands plus a list of public software. The EBVread+ and HHV-7read+ summary statistics (common-variant, HLA-allele and gene-level) are the reusable core of this work. Please deposit them in a public repository such as the GWAS Catalog and release the analysis code, as expected for a genomics Article at this journal.

## Recommended Revisions

1. Discovery and the reported replication are European-only, while All of Us contributes 64,927 African-ancestry and 60,854 Admixed American-ancestry participants used solely for score transferability. Ancestry-stratified results for the 27 non-MHC loci and the HLA alleles, or a trans-ancestry meta-analysis, would materially strengthen the genetic claims.
2. Extended Data Fig. 2 shows effect-size correlation with antibodies against lytic-cycle antigens (p18 VCA r = 0.64, P = 1.55×10⁻⁵; ZEBRA r = 0.45) but not EBNA-1 (r = −0.08), although anti-EBNA-1 is the canonical HLA class II-determined EBV antibody trait (Hammer et al., Am J Hum Genet 97:738, 2015). Interpreting this pattern would sharpen the biology and help justify "latent" in the title.

## Minor Comments

1. The initial UKB cohort is n = 490,293 in the Methods and n = 490,294 in the Results and Fig. 1a.
2. The no-immune-supp cohort is given as n = 305,123, but the variable-selection counts sum to 305,133 (47,234 + 257,899).
3. The serology cohort appears as n = 9,281 (Results), 6,531 (Extended Data Fig. 3a) and 6,065 (Fig. 4b, 4f). Please state the filtering steps producing each.
4. The AoU European replication sample is 184,948 in the Results and 184,949 in Extended Data Fig. 3c; the scDRS level-2 annotation is 21 cell types in the Results and 26 in the Methods.
5. The 5q31.1 locus is IRF1 in Fig. 2a and 5q31.1_SLC22A5 in the fine-mapping Methods. Please harmonize.
6. "28 credible sets of variants (CS; posterior inclusion probability (PIP) >95%)" conflates cumulative credible-set coverage — defined correctly in the Methods — with per-variant PIP.
7. PheWAS: "beta = -0,039" contains a decimal comma. More substantively, the IBD and hypothyroidism coefficients are negative and hypothyroidism has opposite signs for HLA_MHC I and SNP_wo_MHC; please state and interpret the direction of effect, since a negative coefficient does not fit a simple higher-load-higher-risk model.
8. Replication is declared at nominal P < 0.05 across 27 loci and 106 HLA alleles. Please report multiplicity-adjusted replication with directional concordance, and quantify HLA-call concordance between HLA*IMP:02 (UKB) and HLA-TAPAS (AoU), given that 12 of 178 alleles could not be mapped.
9. The Discussion describes "a major (and largely equal) contribution of MHC class I and MHC class II", but Fig. 4b and Extended Data Fig. 3c show class II ΔR² ≈ 0.045–0.047 versus class I ≈ 0.031.
10. The negative-control outcome (red hair colour) includes UKB samples while all other outcome GWAS exclude them; please state whether overlap affects it. Please also copy-edit throughout ("Herefore", "performet", "EBVseron-egative").

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** The association analyses are carefully executed and the resource is valuable, but the phenotype's interpretation as viral load and the causal and disease-overlap claims are not supported at their stated level. Four of the five Major Comments can be addressed with data already in hand or by deposition, so a satisfactory revision is realistic.

## Confidential Comment to Editor

The dataset and question are excellent and the association work is technically strong; my concerns are about interpretation, not execution. The decisive issue is Major 2. The headline novelty — that host control of EBV is causally linked to RA and T1D — rests on instruments that are HLA alleles with direct, established effects on those diseases, and the authors themselves report the effects are MHC-driven with significant heterogeneity. Their Methods state they repeated the analysis excluding MHC SNPs; that result is not in the main text and determines the paper's standing. If it survives, this is a strong Nature Article. If it is null, what remains is a well-executed GWAS of a novel sequencing-derived proxy phenotype plus a descriptive HLA-overlap result — valuable, but in my view better placed in a specialist genetics journal. Major 1 is the second gate: without orthogonal quantification, "viral load" is an inference from a simulation fitted to the observed data. I could not assess the Supplementary Information, which was not provided; parts of Majors 1 and 2 may already be answered there, in which case the required work reduces to relocating and reframing those analyses.
