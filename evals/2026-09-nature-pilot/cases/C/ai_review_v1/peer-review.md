# Referee Report — "Safety and Efficacy of Intratumoral Anti-CTLA4 with Intravenous Anti-PD1" (NIVIPIT, NCT02857569)

## Overall Assessment

This randomized Phase 1b trial compares intratumoral (IT) ipilimumab 0.3 mg/kg with IV ipilimumab 3 mg/kg, both with IV nivolumab, in first-line advanced melanoma (IT n = 40, IV n = 21), with an unusually rich fresh-biopsy biomarker programme. The IT arm met its pre-specified safety threshold and systemic ipilimumab exposure was about 20-fold lower, which is a solid and useful result. The conclusions, however, go well beyond the data: the efficacy claims rest on a comparison that does not isolate the route of administration, while every systemic endpoint numerically favours the IV control; the proposed human Treg-depletion mechanism is contradicted by several of the authors' own panels; and the biomarker associations are stated in terms of necessity and prediction that unadjusted exploratory analyses in small groups cannot support. Once calibrated, the remaining contribution is a sound Phase 1b safety study.

## Major Comments

**1. The efficacy claims are not supported by the comparisons presented.**
The Abstract states that an injected-lesion response of 65.7% versus 50% in non-injected lesions demonstrates "increased efficacy", and the Discussion claims this is "formally demonstrating the added value of anti-CTLA4 on top of anti-PD1 therapy". The comparison varies far more than the local ipilimumab concentration: injected lesions were selected for accessibility (96% lymph node or skin), non-injected target lesions include visceral and liver disease (liver metastases in 18/40 IT patients), the denominators differ (35 vs 40), a single-lesion response is set against a RECIST sum, and there is no anti-PD1-alone comparator. Meanwhile the concurrent randomized control favours IV ipilimumab on every systemic endpoint: non-injected ORR 45–50% vs 62%, progression by new lesion 22.5% vs 9.5%, deaths 21/40 vs 7/21, median PFS 13.8 months vs not reached, and lower BORR in every subgroup of Extended Data Fig. 1a,b. This matters because the proposed clinical use rests on reducing toxicity without losing efficacy, and CheckMate 511 has already shown lower toxicity with reduced-dose IV ipilimumab at descriptively similar ORR (Lebbé et al., J Clin Oncol 2019). Please report all systemic efficacy endpoints side by side in the Abstract and Results with 95% confidence intervals, state explicitly that the trial cannot exclude a clinically relevant loss of efficacy, and remove the "increased efficacy" and "formally demonstrating" claims. A local dose–effect claim would be defensible if supported by a within-patient analysis matching injected and non-injected lesions for site and baseline size under a pre-defined lesion-level criterion (e.g., itRECIST; Goldmacher et al., J Clin Oncol 2020).

**2. The safety conclusions should follow the pre-specified design and are inconsistently reported.**
The primary endpoint appears as 22.6% (Abstract, Discussion) and as 9/37 = 24.3% (Results); the threshold is given as 30% (Results) and 50% (Discussion), whereas the Methods specify p0 = 50%, p1 = 70% and a ≥23/38 rule; and the endpoint is treatment-related grade 3–4 AEs in the Methods but irAEs in the Abstract and Fig. 2a. The Methods state that "No formal comparison between the 2 arms will be performed", yet the Abstract reports a "significantly lower incidence", and "equivalent to anti-PD1 monotherapy" is a cross-trial inference (CheckMate 067 nivolumab alone: 16.3% grade 3–4 TRAEs). Because the endpoint itself appears robust (28/40 would still pass the rule), this is correctable by reporting. Please report the primary endpoint exactly as pre-specified with its confidence interval, add a sensitivity analysis counting the three excluded early deaths and the two randomized but non-analysed IT patients (Fig. 1c) as failures, give a per-patient table of worst-grade TRAEs by arm including any grade 5 event, describe the between-arm difference as descriptive, and remove the monotherapy-equivalence claim.

**3. The Treg-depletion mechanism is not demonstrated and is partly contradicted by the data.**
The claim that anti-CTLA4 works "in humans just like in mice by depleting activated tumor-specific Tregs" rests on a decrease in CD25+CD39+ cells as a proportion of CD4+ cells in DCB patients, with arms pooled (Fig. 6e, p = 0.032). Other panels point the other way: the same change as a proportion of CD45+ cells is not significant (Extended Data Fig. 6f, p = 0.053, although the text calls it significant); the CTLA4+ subset, the proposed target, does not change in DCB (Extended Data Fig. 6g, p = 0.415); Treg deconvolution increases (Extended Data Fig. 6d); FOXP3 IHC and the CD8/FOXP3 ratio are unchanged (Extended Data Fig. 6e; Fig. 6f); and circulating Tregs rise (Extended Data Fig. 6h). A relative decrease during a documented effector influx (Extended Data Fig. 5c,d) cannot distinguish depletion from dilution, from CD25/CD39 downregulation, or from differences between the lesions sampled at baseline and W3, and FcγR transcripts are associated with outcome but never linked to Treg change within patients. The manuscript therefore does not resolve the standing controversy (Sharma et al., Clin Cancer Res 2019; Arce Vargas et al., Cancer Cell 2018; Romano et al., PNAS 2015). To retain the claim, please provide absolute densities (cells/mm²) of FOXP3+ and, if possible, CD25+ or CTLA4+ Treg cells in paired sections of the same lesion type — multiplex IHC on the existing FFPE cores would suffice — shown by arm and by injected versus non-injected lesion, since depletion should track local ipilimumab exposure, together with a within-patient test of whether FcγR+/CD16+ myeloid density predicts the change in Tregs. Otherwise, please describe the finding as a relative decrease compatible with, but not demonstrating, depletion, and remove the mouse-equivalence statement and the "Selective … Depletion" heading.

**4. The biomarker analyses support associations, not necessity or prediction.**
The Results header states that MHC-I/II immunity is "required" for DCB, the Abstract calls the regulatory–effector interface "a pre-requisite" and describes features as "predicting" benefit, and the Discussion calls the fresh-biopsy assays "robust predictive biomarkers". The analysis cannot carry this: markers across five platforms were compared with unadjusted Wilcoxon tests without per-group n, the arms are pooled although the biopsied lesion differs by arm and time point, W3 changes may partly reflect early response to an outcome defined at 6 months, and several readouts described as higher or confirmatory are not significant (HLA-I IHC p = 0.080, HLA-II IHC p = 0.054, CD20 IHC p = 0.084, CD68 IHC p = 0.169, CD163 IHC p = 0.221, and the W3 granzyme secretome increase cited in the Abstract, p ≈ 0.09–0.10). The Treg and M2 findings are also framed as unexpected, although high baseline FoxP3 has been associated with ipilimumab activity (Hamid et al., J Transl Med 2011) and Tregs and PD-L1 are recruited as a consequence of CD8-driven inflammation (Spranger et al., Sci Transl Med 2013), so these features may simply mark inflamed tumours; Romano et al. additionally reported a higher CD68/CD163 ratio in responders, which should be discussed. Please report n per group for each panel, apply false-discovery-rate control within pre-defined marker families while separating pre-specified from exploratory analyses, stratify or adjust by arm and lesion type, test whether the Treg and M2 associations survive normalization to total immune or T-cell-inflamed content, and report cross-validated AUCs with confidence intervals before using "predictive". Otherwise, please revise the language throughout to "associated with".

## Recommended Revisions

1. Where trial material allows, quantify the FOXP3+ fraction within the CD25+CD39+ gate; the surrogate is currently validated in seven external tumours (Extended Data Fig. 6a).
2. If germline DNA is available, FCGR3A-158V/F genotyping would provide an independent test of the Fc-dependence proposed in Major Comment 3.
3. Deposit RNA-seq and WES data in a controlled-access repository and release analysis code; the present statement covers clinical data on request only.

## Minor Comments

1. Please reconcile: non-injected ORR 18/40 = 45% (Results) vs 50% (Abstract, Discussion, Fig. 3h); IV ORR 62% vs ~65%; "13/40 (37.5%)" early ipilimumab discontinuation (13/40 = 32.5%); 46 injected lesions vs ≥48 implied by 65% one lesion and 27.5% two; grade 3 irAEs 33%/62% (text) vs 30%/52.3% (Fig. 2b); median OS 50 months (text) vs "not reached for the 2 arms" (Fig. 3 legend); and "TOTAL (n=149)" in Fig. 5k.
2. Per CONSORT and the journal's clinical research policy, please give exact dates of first and last enrolment and of data lock, registration timing relative to first enrolment, ORR confidence intervals, the pre-specified irRC results, and the reason the analysis was not by intention to treat.
3. Please specify the injection procedure: volume, how 0.3 mg/kg was divided across lesions, and whether an injected lesion could also be a RECIST target lesion.
4. The Abstract describes patients as "untreated", but BRAF-mutant patients could have received prior BRAF/MEK therapy; please state how many did.
5. Figure references need correction: "Fig. 2i" should be Fig. 2g (right); the CD4/CD8 panels are swapped between text and the Fig. 4a / Extended Data Fig. 2a legends; PK is called serum in the text and plasma in the figure; "Stade" in Fig. 1d; the DCB colour key in Extended Data Fig. 3a is inverted relative to other figures; Methods Figure 1 and Methods Table 2 are missing from the submitted file.
6. PD-1 downregulation on CD8+ TILs is seen only in the IT arm (Fig. 4a; IV p = 0.834). Please discuss whether this reflects sampling of injected lesions rather than nivolumab target engagement.
7. The flow panel changed after nine patients; please state how many patients per group have CD39 data.
8. Extended Data Fig. 1d (OS by DCB) is subject to guarantee-time bias, since DCB requires survival to 6 months; please use a landmark analysis and account for the six patients missing from 36 + 19.
9. References 50–53 appear uncited and unrelated. Ray et al. (2016) reported an irRC ORR of 40% and an abscopal response in 89%; the text presents 40% as the abscopal response rate.

## Recommendation

Reject in its current form. The safety and pharmacokinetic result is valid and clinically useful, but the two claims that would give the work broad significance — preserved or increased efficacy with intratumoral delivery, and a human Treg-depletion mechanism — are not supported. The first cannot be resolved within this trial, and the second would require new analyses with an uncertain outcome.

## Confidential Comment to Editor

This is a carefully executed randomized Phase 1b trial with exceptional prospective fresh-tissue sampling, and the primary result (pre-specified safety threshold met, systemic ipilimumab exposure roughly 20-fold lower) is genuine and publishable. The blocker is the gap between those data and the headline claims, not the validity of the safety data: efficacy is descriptively worse than the concurrent IV arm on every systemic endpoint and the trial cannot answer the efficacy question, while the Treg-depletion conclusion is contradicted by several of the authors' own panels. Calibrated, the paper is a Phase 1b safety report with exploratory associations that largely confirm known correlates of checkpoint-blockade benefit, which in my view does not reach the threshold for this journal even after a strong revision; it would suit a clinical or translational oncology journal well, and transfer would be reasonable. If absolute Treg quantification by lesion and arm unexpectedly supported depletion with a clear Fc link, the mechanistic component would become substantially stronger.
